All thirty-six, as text

  • Friedrich Nietzsche

    On the Genealogy of Morality (1887)

    “While every noble morality develops from a triumphant affirmation of itself, slave morality from the outset says No to what is ‘outside,’ what is ‘different,’ what is ‘not itself’; and this No is its creative deed.”

  • G. W. F. Hegel

    Phenomenology of Spirit (1807)

    “The True is the whole. But the whole is only the essence completing itself through its development. Of the absolute it must be said that it is essentially result, that only in the end is it what it is in truth; and precisely in this consists its nature: to be actual, subject, or becoming-itself.”

  • Karl Marx

    Capital (1867)

    “The wealth of those societies in which the capitalist mode of production prevails, presents itself as ‘an immense accumulation of commodities,’ its unit being a single commodity.”

  • Max Weber

    Economy and Society (1922)

    “Sociology… is a science concerning itself with the interpretive understanding of social action and thereby with a causal explanation of its course and consequences. We shall speak of ‘action’ insofar as the acting individual attaches a subjective meaning to his behavior—be it overt or covert, omission or acquiescence.”

  • Gaston Bachelard

    The Formation of the Scientific Mind (1938)

    “In the formation of a scientific mind, the first obstacle is primary experience, the experience placed before and above criticism, which is rich and gaudy.”

  • Pierre Bourdieu

    The Craft of Sociology (1968); Distinction (1979)

    “Taste classifies, and it classifies the classifier.”

  • Michel Foucault

    Discipline and Punish (1975)

    “The ‘Enlightenment’, which discovered the liberties, also invented the disciplines.”

  • Loïc Wacquant

    Urban Outcasts (2008); Punishing the Poor (2009)

    “It partakes of a broader reconstruction of the state wedding restrictive ‘workfare’ and expansive ‘prisonfare’ under a philosophy of moral behaviorism.”

  • Gilles Deleuze

    Nietzsche and Philosophy (1962); Difference and Repetition (1968)

    “A rhizome has no beginning or end; it is always in the middle, between things, interbeing, intermezzo.”

  • Félix Guattari

    The Three Ecologies (1989); Chaosmosis (1992)

    “Here we are talking about a reconstruction of social and individual practices which I shall classify under three complementary headings, all of which come under the ethico-aesthetic aegis of an ecosophy: social ecology, mental ecology and environmental ecology.”

  • Jacques Derrida

    Of Grammatology (1967); “Cogito and the History of Madness” (1963)

    “There is nothing outside the text.”

  • Jean-François Lyotard

    The Postmodern Condition (1979); The Differend (1983)

    “Simplifying to the extreme, I define postmodern as incredulity towards metanarratives.”

  • Simone de Beauvoir

    The Second Sex (1949)

    “One is not born, but rather becomes, a woman.”

  • Carl Jung

    Psychological Types (1921); The Archetypes and the Collective Unconscious (1959)

    “A more or less superficial layer of the unconscious is undoubtedly personal. But this personal unconscious rests upon a deeper layer, which does not derive from personal experience and is not a personal acquisition but is inborn. This deeper layer I call the collective unconscious.”

  • Marie-Louise von Franz

    The Interpretation of Fairy Tales (1970)

    “To me the fairy tale is like the sea, and the sagas and myths are like the waves upon it; a tale rises to be a myth and sinks down again into being a fairy tale.”

  • Antonio Gramsci

    Prison Notebooks (1929–1935)

    “The supremacy of a social group manifests itself in two ways, as ‘domination’ and as ‘intellectual and moral leadership.’”

  • Frantz Fanon

    Black Skin, White Masks (1952); The Wretched of the Earth (1961)

    “The black man has no ontological resistance in the eyes of the white man.”

  • Stuart Hall

    “Encoding/Decoding” (1973); Policing the Crisis (1978)

    “There is no intelligible discourse without the operation of a code.”

  • Michael Burawoy

    Manufacturing Consent (1979); “For Public Sociology” (2005)

    “It is not the extraction of surplus value but its obscuring—together with the securing of surplus value—that constitutes the basis of capitalist control over the labor process.”

  • Henri Lefebvre

    The Production of Space (1974)

    “(Social) space is a (social) product.”

  • Erik Olin Wright

    Classes (1985); Envisioning Real Utopias (2010)

    “An emancipatory social science needs a systematic diagnosis and critique of the world as it exists, a coherent theory of alternatives, and an account of possible transformations.”

  • Charles Tilly

    Durable Inequality (1998)

    “Large, significant inequalities in advantages among human beings correspond mainly to categorical differences such as black/white, male/female, citizen/foreigner, or Muslim/Jew rather than to individual differences in attributes, propensities, or performances.”

  • W. E. B. Du Bois

    The Philadelphia Negro (1899); Black Reconstruction (1935)

    “It is a peculiar sensation, this double-consciousness, this sense of always looking at one’s self through the eyes of others, of measuring one’s soul by the tape of a world that looks on in amused contempt and pity.”

  • Derrick Bell

    “Brown v. Board and the Interest-Convergence Dilemma” (1980)

    “The interest of blacks in achieving racial equality will be accommodated only when it converges with the interests of whites.”

  • Kimberlé Crenshaw

    “Demarginalizing the Intersection” (1989)

    “Consider an analogy to traffic in an intersection, coming and going in all four directions. Discrimination, like traffic through an intersection, may flow in one direction, and it may flow in another. If an accident happens in an intersection, it can be caused by cars traveling from any number of directions and, sometimes, from all of them.”

  • Cheryl Harris

    “Whiteness as Property” (1993)

    “Whiteness as property has finally severed itself from the white body. What remains is the concept of whiteness as the right to exclude, or to be protected from, the encroachments of others.”

  • Patricia Hill Collins

    Black Feminist Thought (1990)

    “Suppressing the knowledge produced by any oppressed group makes it easier for dominant groups to rule because the seeming absence of dissent suggests that subordinate groups willingly collaborate in their own victimization.”

  • Saidiya Hartman

    Scenes of Subjection (1997); Lose Your Mother (2007)

    “The value of blackness resided in its metaphorical aptitude, whether literally understood as the fungibility of the commodity or understood as the imaginative surface upon which the master and the nation came to understand themselves.”

  • Dorothy Roberts

    Shattered Bonds (2002); Torn Apart (2022)

    “The child welfare system is designed to detect and punish neglect on the part of poor parents and to ignore most middle-class and wealthy parents’ failings.”

  • Ruth Wilson Gilmore

    Golden Gulag (2007)

    “Racism, specifically, is the state-sanctioned or extralegal production and exploitation of group-differentiated vulnerability to premature death.”

  • Roy Bhaskar

    A Realist Theory of Science (1975); The Possibility of Naturalism (1979)

    “Science, then, is the systematic attempt to express in thought the structures and ways of acting of things that exist and act independently of us.”

  • Margaret Archer

    Realist Social Theory (1995); Structure, Agency and the Internal Conversation (2003)

    “Since structure and agency constitute different levels of stratified social reality, each possesses distinctive emergent properties which are real and causally efficacious but irreducible to one another.”

  • George Herbert Mead

    Mind, Self, and Society (1934)

    “The self is not initially there, at birth, but arises in the process of social experience and activity, that is, develops in the given individual as a result of his relations to that process as a whole and to other individuals within that process.”

  • Herbert Blumer

    Symbolic Interactionism: Perspective and Method (1969)

    “Human beings act toward things on the basis of the meanings that the things have for them; the meaning of such things is derived from, or arises out of, the social interaction one has with one’s fellows; these meanings are handled in, and modified through, an interpretive process used by the person in dealing with the things he encounters.”

  • Erving Goffman

    The Presentation of Self in Everyday Life (1959); Stigma (1963)

    “When an individual enters the presence of others, they commonly seek to acquire information about him or to bring into play information about him already possessed.”

  • Karl Polanyi

    The Great Transformation (1944)

    “Our thesis is that the idea of a self-adjusting market implied a stark utopia. Such an institution could not exist for any length of time without annihilating the human and natural substance of society.”

  • Zygmunt Bauman

    Modernity and the Holocaust (1989); Liquid Modernity (2000)

    “Fluids travel easily. They ‘flow’, ‘spill’, ‘run out’, ‘splash’, ‘pour over’, ‘leak’, ‘flood’, ‘spray’, ‘drip’, ‘seep’, ‘ooze’; unlike solids, they are not easily stopped—they pass around some obstacles, dissolve some others and bore or soak their way through others still.”

  • Gary Alan Fine

    Kitchens: The Culture of Restaurant Work (1996); Tiny Publics (2012)

    “Groups are the crucible in which culture is created, tested, and transformed; they link the micro-order of interaction to the macro-order of institutions.”
